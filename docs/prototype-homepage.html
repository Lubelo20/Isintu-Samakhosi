Isintu Samakhosi Institution is a Non-Profit Company building the economic, institutional and cultural infrastructure that lets rural communities generate their own wealth and govern their own affairs.

We work in partnership with chiefdoms and kingdoms across all nine provinces of South Africa. Communities become co-owners of their economic future, not dependants.

How we do it

The Ubuntu Triad

Three commitments shape who we serve, how we work and what we protect.

Abantu

The people

Our first loyalty is to the communities we serve: the rural poor, indigenous peoples, women and girls, youth, and those living at the margins. Every decision begins and ends with one question. Does this serve the people?

Ubuntu

I am because we are

Ubuntu is not sentiment. It is a governance system. Relationships built on dignity, reciprocity and mutual accountability are the infrastructure of all our work.

Isintu

Our custom and way of life

We honour and protect indigenous knowledge, customary law and the governance sovereignty of Amakhosi. The legitimacy of our work is inseparable from the legitimacy of traditional leadership.

Building infrastructure, not dependency

Eight programmes tackling systemic inequality in township, village and rural economies.

Every programme must answer three questions:

  • ConsciousnessDoes it build critical awareness and self-determination?
  • HeritageDoes it revitalise indigenous knowledge and Amakhosi governance?
  • StewardshipDoes it manage resources with accountability to future generations?

    A GBV centre in every traditional council

    South Africa has one of the highest rates of gender-based violence in the world, and rural communities remain underserved. Traditional courts are the first place many people turn, yet they lack accredited training and referral pathways.

    We are changing that by establishing GBV centres inside traditional councils, each backed by a community User Group that acts as the eyes, ears and accountability of the Institution on the ground.

    25traditional councils in year one

    No centre opens until the groundwork is done

    Among the eight pre-deployment documents filed for every centre:

    1. Community baseline assessmentUnderstanding the community before we act in it.
    2. Traditional Council ResolutionThe council formally invites and owns the centre.
    3. Referral pathway mapClear routes to police, health and social services.
    4. Risk and safety planProtecting survivors, staff and the User Group.
